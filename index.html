AUDIT #1: 19–20 OCTOBER 2026 (INDEPENDENT REVIEW)

1. Legal Inaccuracy on Secret Spousal Recordings (Essay #007 & #002)

What was written: Claimed secret phone recordings of a spouse are 'routinely thrown out' by Indian family courts due to privacy violations, citing an unverified case name ('Vibhor Garg v. Neha (Supreme Court of India, 2025)').
The Reality: The Supreme Court held in Vibhor Garg v. Neha (14 July 2025) that secret spousal phone recordings are admissible in matrimonial disputes under Indian law, though they require proof under Section 65B of the Evidence Act / Section 63 BSA and must establish authenticity.
Action Taken: Purged the unverified case name, corrected the legal holding across Essay #007 and #002, and clearly documented both admissibility and practical evidential burdens.

2. Spurious Precision & Pop-Psychology Numbers (Essays #002, #003, #005)

What was written: Cited '91.2% Gottman divorce accuracy', '240% increase in contact dermatitis', '4,000 ASCI violations', and treated birth-order archetypes as a 'Clinical Matrix'.
The Reality: The Gottman lab's accuracy claims come from retrospective, small-sample models that fail to hold up to uniform 91.2% precision. Large-scale empirical psychology (Rohrer et al., 2015, PNAS) showed negligible birth-order effects on Big Five personality traits.
Action Taken: Stripped out false decimal precision, demoted birth-order claims from 'clinical proof' to observational family systems patterns, and noted empirical caveats.

3. Defamatory / Inaccurate Regulatory Descriptions (Essays #003 & #004)

What was written: Claimed SEBI 'seized ₹6.07 Crore from P.R. Sundar for deceptive schemes'.
The Reality: Sundar settled with SEBI in May 2023 under consent terms without admitting or denying findings over unregistered investment advice.
Action Taken: Corrected the language to accurately reflect regulatory settlement terminology. Replaced unverified creator income gossip with systemic business model mechanics.

4. Mathematical Inconsistency in Debt Calculations (Essay #004)

What was written: Claimed an '₹18 Lakh mortgage penalty' between 8.50% and 9.75% home loan rates on a ₹60 Lakh loan.
The Reality: ₹76.59 Lakh minus ₹64.97 Lakh equals exactly ₹11.62 Lakh. The headline exaggerated the cost by nearly ₹6.4 Lakh.
Action Taken: Recalculated all figures in code and synced headlines with the exact mathematical difference.
